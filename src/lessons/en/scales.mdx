There are 12 notes per octave. A **scale** picks 7 (or 5) of them. A **key** is a scale plus a home note, the **tonic**. Pick a key at the start of a track, stay inside it, and almost nothing you play will sound wrong.

## Why limit yourself to 7 notes?

The notes of a scale are chosen so that most pairs form simple ratios (fifths, fourths, thirds — lesson 2). Notes outside the scale form more complex ratios with the tonic and with each other, so they sound like friction. A key is a pre-filtered set of notes that work together.

## Scales are step patterns

A scale is defined by its **steps**, not its notes. W = whole step (2 semitones), H = half step (1 semitone). Start the same pattern on any note and you get that scale in a new key.

| Scale | Steps | Mood | Where you hear it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Major | W W H W W W H | bright, happy | piano house, uplifting trance, festival EDM |
| Natural minor | W H W W H W W | dark, emotional | most house, techno, trance, EDM, bounce, hard house |
| Dorian | W H W W W H W | minor but warmer (raised 6th) | deep house, soulful grooves |
| Phrygian | H W W W H W W | very dark, tense (flat 2nd) | hard/techy riffs, the psytrance example in lesson 23 |
| Minor pentatonic | 5 notes: 0 3 5 7 10 | can't-go-wrong minor | basslines, simple hooks |

## Scale degrees: numbers instead of note names

In Strudel, `n()` with `.scale()` counts **steps inside the scale**, starting at 0 (the tonic). `0 1 2 3 4 5 6` are the 7 notes, `7` is the tonic an octave up, `-1` is one scale step below the tonic.

<Strudel title="up and down the scale" code={`
  n("0 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 6 5 4 3 2 1 0 -1")
    .scale("A3:minor")
    .s("triangle")
    ._pianoroll()
`} />

The big win: change one string and the whole melody moves to a new key or mood, and stays correct.

<Strudel title="same melody, four scales" code={`
  n("0 2 4 7 6 4 2 1")
    .scale("<A3:minor A3:major A3:dorian A3:phrygian>")
    .s("sawtooth").lpf(1500).decay(0.2).sustain(0.3)
    ._pianoroll()
`} />

## One key for the whole track

Put the key in a variable and use it everywhere. Single quotes make it a plain text value (double quotes would turn it into a pattern).

<Strudel title="bass + melody sharing one key" code={`
  setcpm(124/4)
  const key = 'A:minor'
  bass: n("<0 0 5 4>*4").scale(key).transpose(-24) // same key, 2 octaves down
    .s("sawtooth").lpf(400).decay(0.15).sustain(0)
  lead: n("<[0 2 4 2] [4 ~ 2 ~] [5 4 2 0] [1 ~ 0 ~]>*2")
    .scale(key).s("square").lpf(2000).gain(0.4)
    .decay(0.15).sustain(0)
  drums: s("bd*4, [~ oh]*4, ~ cp ~ cp").bank("RolandTR909")
`} />

<Callout kind="tip">
Change `'A:minor'` to `'F:minor'` or `'C:dorian'` and press Update. Everything follows. This is how you test which key suits a sound before committing.
</Callout>

## Home and away: tension in a scale

Not all scale notes feel the same against the tonic.

| Degree (in `n`) | Name | Feeling over the tonic |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | tonic (1st) | home, resolved |
| 4 | fifth | stable, strong |
| 2 | third | stable, sets major/minor mood |
| 3 | fourth | slightly hanging |
| 5 | sixth | colour (dorian raises it → warmer) |
| 1 | second | passing, wants to move |
| 6 | seventh | tension, wants to resolve up to the tonic |

End a phrase on 0, 2 or 4 and it sounds finished. End on 1 or 6 and it sounds like a question. Hooks often ask the question in bar 2 and answer it in bar 4.

<Strudel title="question… answer" code={`
  n("<[0 2 4 2] [4 5 6 ~] [0 2 4 2] [4 2 1 0]>")
    .scale("A3:minor").s("triangle")
    ._pianoroll()
`} />
