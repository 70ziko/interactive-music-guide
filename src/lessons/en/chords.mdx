A **chord** is three or more notes played together. In house, chords are the emotional layer: a minor 7th stab on the offbeat is half of the genre's sound. Other genres use chords differently: **techno** often has one chord or none (a dub stab at most), **trance** holds each chord for a whole bar and chops it with a gate, and **EDM** loops four big pop chords under the drop.

## Building a chord: stack thirds

Take any scale degree, skip one, take the next, skip one, take the next. In scale-degree numbers that is **0, 2, 4**. The result is a **triad**.

Whether it is major or minor depends on the gaps, counted in semitones:

| Chord | Gaps (semitones) | Symbol | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| Major | 4 + 3 | `C` | bright, stable |
| Minor | 3 + 4 | `Cm` | dark, soft |
| Minor 7th | 3 + 4 + 3 | `Cm7` | smooth, the classic house chord |
| Minor 9th | 3 + 4 + 3 + 4 | `Cm9` | lush, deep house |
| Major 7th | 4 + 3 + 4 | `CM7` / `C^7` | dreamy, open |
| Dominant 7th | 4 + 3 + 3 | `C7` | bluesy, wants to move |
| Sus2 / Sus4 | 2 + 5 / 5 + 2 | `Csus2` | open, neither major nor minor |

## Chords from the scale

If you build every triad from the scale's own notes, you get the **diatonic chords** of the key. They all fit together. We name them with Roman numerals: uppercase = major, lowercase = minor, ° = diminished.

| Key of A minor | i | ii° | III | iv | v | VI | VII |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chord | Am | Bdim | C | Dm | Em | F | G |
| Degrees `n` | 0,2,4 | 1,3,5 | 2,4,6 | 3,5,7 | 4,6,8 | 5,7,9 | 6,8,10 |

Strudel builds them for you: write the degrees inside `[ , ]` and `.scale()` picks the right notes.

<Strudel title="i – VI – III – VII in A minor" code={`
  setcpm(124/4)
  n("<[0,2,4] [5,7,9] [2,4,6] [6,8,10]>")
    .scale("A3:minor")
    .s("sawtooth").lpf(1400).gain(0.5)
    ._pianoroll()
`} />

That progression — **i – VI – III – VII** (Am F C G) — is one of the most used minor progressions in dance music. Other safe loops in a minor key:

| Progression | In A minor | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| i – VI – III – VII | Am F C G | epic, anthemic — festival EDM, trance |
| i – VII – VI – VII | Am G F G | driving, rave |
| i – iv | Am Dm | hypnotic two-chord vamp, very house |
| i – VI | Am F | melancholic, breakdown-ready |
| i (one chord) | Am7 | deep house, techno: groove does the work |

## The circle of fifths

Arrange the 12 keys so each step is a fifth up (×3/2). Neighbours share 6 of their 7 notes. The chords of any key sit in a small wedge: the key, its two neighbours, and their relative minors.

Use it to answer "which chords go with this one?" — look at the wedge. And to move between keys smoothly: neighbours on the circle sound related.

## Voicing: how to stack the notes

The same chord can be played with its notes in different orders and octaves. Jumping from one root-position chord to the next sounds clumsy. Moving each note **the shortest distance** sounds smooth — this is called **voice leading**. Strudel's `voicing()` does it for you.

<Strudel title="chord symbols + automatic voicing" code={`
  setcpm(124/4)
  chord("<Am7 Dm7 FM7 Em7>")
    .voicing()
    .s("gm_epiano1").gain(0.7)
    ._pianoroll()
`} />

## The house stab

Short chords, rhythmic, usually on offbeats or syncopated 16ths. `.struct()` gives the chord a rhythm: `x` = play, `~` = rest.

<Strudel title="house organ stabs" code={`
  setcpm(124/4)
  stabs: chord("<Am7 Am7 Dm7 Em7>")
    .voicing()
    .struct("[~ x ~ ~] [x ~ ~ x] [~ ~ x ~] [~ x ~ ~]")
    .s("gm_drawbar_organ").decay(0.18).sustain(0).gain(0.8)
  drums: s("bd*4, [~ oh]*4, ~ cp ~ cp").bank("RolandTR909")
  bass: n("<0 0 3 4>*8").scale("A1:minor").struct("[~ x]*4")
    .s("sawtooth").lpf(350).decay(0.12).sustain(0)
`} />

<Strudel title="classic offbeat saw stabs (more rave)" code={`
  setcpm(128/4)
  chord("<Am Am F G>")
    .voicing()
    .struct("[~ x]*4")
    .s("sawtooth").lpf(2200).lpq(4)
    .decay(0.12).sustain(0).room(0.3)
    ._pianoroll()
`} />
