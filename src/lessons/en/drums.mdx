House, bounce and hard house share one drum skeleton. Learn its four rules and you can write a working beat in under a minute, then spend your time on what makes it yours.

## The four rules

| # | Rule | Steps (of 16) | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Kick on every beat** ("four on the floor") | 1, 5, 9, 13 | The steady pulse dancers lock onto |
| 2 | **Clap or snare on beats 2 and 4** (the backbeat) | 5, 13 | Answers the kick, gives the bar a shape |
| 3 | **Open hat on every offbeat** ("&") | 3, 7, 11, 15 | Fills the gap between kicks → the bounce, the "pump" |
| 4 | **Closed hats on 16ths** (optional, quieter) | most steps | Drive and texture |

Rule 3 is the heart of the genre: kick–hat–kick–hat is the "oom-tss" you know. In bounce and hard house the offbeat is often played by the **bass** as well (lesson 9).

## Groove: the space between "correct" and "alive"

A machine-perfect grid sounds stiff. Three tools fix that.

### 1. Dynamics (velocity)

Not every hit should be equally loud. Accent the offbeat hats, keep the in-between 16ths quiet ("ghost notes").

<Strudel title="hat dynamics" code={`
  setcpm(124/4)
  stack(
    s("bd*4"),
    s("hh*16").gain("[0.25 0.35 0.7 0.35]*4"),  // per beat: quiet-quiet-LOUD-quiet
  ).bank("RolandTR909")._punchcard()
`} />

### 2. Swing (shuffle)

Swing delays every second 16th a little. `swingBy(x, n)` splits the cycle into `n` slices and delays the second half of each slice by `x` of a half slice. With `n = 8` each slice is an 8th note, so the delayed half is the "e" and "a" 16ths.

<Callout kind="math">
Straight 16ths sit at 0 and ½ of each 8th. With `swingBy(1/3, 8)` the second one moves to ½ + ⅓·½ = **⅔**. The pair now splits 2 : 1 — a triplet feel. Smaller values (1/6, 1/8) give a subtle "humanised" lilt; 1/3 is a full shuffle.
</Callout>

<Strudel title="swing on / off every 2 bars" code={`
  setcpm(122/4)
  stack(
    s("bd*4, ~ cp ~ cp"),
    s("hh*16").gain("[0.3 0.5]*8"),
    s("[~ oh]*4").gain(0.5)
  ).bank("RolandTR909")
   .swingBy("<0 0 0.33 0.33>", 8)
`} />

### 3. Euclidean rhythms

`x(k,n)` spreads `k` hits as evenly as possible over `n` steps. Many traditional rhythms are Euclidean: `(3,8)` is the Cuban tresillo, `(5,8)` the cinquillo. They are great for percussion that sits *against* the straight kick.

<Strudel title="euclid percussion" code={`
  setcpm(124/4)
  stack(
    s("bd*4, ~ cp ~ cp, [~ oh]*4").bank("RolandTR909"),
    s("rim(3,8)").bank("RolandTR909").gain(0.6),
    s("<~ cb(5,16)>").bank("RolandTR808").gain(0.4)
  )._punchcard()
`} />

## Fills: tell the listener a section is ending

Dance music moves in 4- and 8-bar phrases (lesson 13). A small change in the **last bar** of a phrase — a clap roll, a dropped kick, an extra hat — tells the listener "something new is coming". `<…>` makes this easy: give it 4 values, change only the last one.

<Strudel title="fill every 4th bar" code={`
  setcpm(124/4)
  stack(
    s("bd*4"),
    s("<[~ cp]*2 [~ cp]*2 [~ cp]*2 [~ cp ~ [cp cp]]>"),
    s("[~ oh]*4").gain(0.5),
    s("<~ ~ ~ [~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ sd sd sd sd]>").gain(0.6)
  ).bank("RolandTR909")._punchcard()
`} />

## Harder: towards hard house

Hard house and bounce keep the same skeleton but push it: faster, a distorted kick, the snare more prominent, rolls into every drop. `distort` adds harmonics to the kick (lesson 1: more harmonics = brighter, more aggressive).

<Strudel title="hard kick" code={`
  setcpm(145/4)
  stack(
    s("bd*4").distort("<0 1 2 3>").gain(0.8),
    s("~ cp ~ cp"),
    s("[~ oh]*4").gain(0.55),
    s("hh*16").gain(0.25)
  ).bank("RolandTR909")
`} />

| | House | Bounce / scouse | Hard house |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tempo (roughly) | 120–128 | 140–150 | 140–150 |
| Kick | round, clean | punchy | hard, distorted |
| Offbeat | open hat | open hat **and** bass ("donk") | open hat and bass stabs |
| Swing | often | rare | rare |
| Fills | subtle | snare rolls | long snare rolls into drops |
