Almost every synth sound in electronic dance music — house, techno, trance, EDM — comes from one recipe called **subtractive synthesis**: start with a sound full of harmonics, then carve it. Learn the four stages and you can read (and rebuild) any synth patch.

## 1. Oscillator: pick your harmonics

From lesson 1: sine = 1 harmonic, triangle/square = odd harmonics, saw = all of them. Two more sources matter for dance music:

- **supersaw** — several saws slightly out of tune with each other. The detune makes them beat slowly (lesson 2), which sounds wide and huge. Trance leads, hard house stabs and hoover-style sounds start here.
- **noise** (`white`, `pink`) — every frequency at once. Hats, risers, the "breath" in a sound.

<Strudel title="saw vs supersaw" code={`
  note("<[a2,e3,a3] [a2,e3,a3]>")
    .s("<sawtooth supersaw>")
    .lpf(3000).gain(0.5)
    .sustain(1)
    ._spectrum()
`} />

<Strudel title="supersaw: more voices, more detune" code={`
  note("a3").s("supersaw")
    .unison("<1 3 7 7>")       // how many saws
    .detune("<0 0.2 0.2 0.6>") // how far apart they are tuned
    .lpf(4000).gain(0.4).sustain(1)
`} />

## 2. Filter: carve the harmonics

A **low-pass filter** (`lpf`) lets low frequencies through and removes harmonics above the **cutoff**. **Resonance** (`lpq`) boosts a narrow band right at the cutoff — the squelchy, vocal "wow" sound.

Press **Play saw**. Sweep the cutoff from 16 kHz down to 100 Hz: bright → dark → muffled. Now set resonance to ~12 and sweep again: that whistling peak is what acid house and acid techno are built on. Turn on the **LFO** to let a sine wave move the cutoff for you.

## 3. Envelope: loudness over time

Every note has a shape in time. **ADSR**: how fast it starts (Attack), how fast it falls to the held level (Decay), the held level (Sustain), how long it rings after the note ends (Release).

<AdsrGraph />

| Sound | A | D | S | R |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pluck / stab | ~0 | short | 0 | short |
| Bass | ~0 | short | high | very short |
| Pad | slow | medium | high | long |
| Donk | 0 | very short | 0 | 0 |

## 4. Modulation: make knobs move

A static sound gets boring in 8 bars. **Modulation** moves a parameter automatically.

- An **LFO** (low-frequency oscillator) is a slow wave used as a hand on a knob. In Strudel, `sine`, `saw`, `tri`, `square` and `perlin` (smooth random) are continuous patterns: `sine.range(300, 3000).slow(8)` goes from 300 to 3000 and back over 8 bars.
- A **filter envelope** opens the filter at the start of each note and closes it again: `lpenv` sets how far (in octaves), `lpattack`/`lpdecay` set how fast.

<Strudel title="LFO on the cutoff (slow sweep)" code={`
  setcpm(124/4)
  note("[~ a1]*4").s("sawtooth")
    .lpf(sine.range(200, 2500).slow(8))
    .lpq(6).decay(0.2).sustain(0.3)
    ._scope()
`} />

<Strudel title="filter envelope: same note, different lpenv" code={`
  setcpm(124/4)
  note("a2*8").s("sawtooth")
    .lpf(300).lpq(8)
    .lpenv("<0 2 4 6>")      // how many octaves the filter opens per note
    .lpdecay(0.12)
    .decay(0.2).sustain(0)
`} />

## Recipes

These combine all four stages. Treat them as starting points: change one number at a time and listen.

### Acid line (TB-303 style)

Saw + high resonance + filter envelope + a slowly moving cutoff + some drive. Accent and slide are what make it talk; here the pattern of octaves and rests does most of that work.

<Strudel title="acid" code={`
  setcpm(126/4)
  acid: note("a1 a2 a1 [a1 c2] a1 [~ a2] e2 [a1 g2]")
    .s("sawtooth").ftype("ladder")
    .lpf(sine.range(300, 1800).slow(16)).lpq(14)
    .lpenv(3).lpdecay(0.1)
    .decay(0.15).sustain(0.2)
    .distort(0.8).gain(0.6)
  kick: s("bd*4").bank("RolandTR909")
`} />

### Chord pluck

<Strudel title="pluck" code={`
  setcpm(124/4)
  chord("<Am7 FM7>").voicing()
    .struct("[~ x ~ x] [~ ~ x ~]")
    .s("sawtooth").lpf(600).lpenv(4).lpdecay(0.08)
    .decay(0.2).sustain(0).room(0.3)
    ._pianoroll()
`} />

### Pad

Slow attack, long release, filtered supersaw, lots of reverb. Pads sit behind everything and fill breakdowns.

<Strudel title="pad" code={`
  chord("<Am9 FM7>/2").voicing()
    .s("supersaw").unison(5).detune(0.3)
    .attack(0.8).release(2).sustain(0.8)
    .lpf(1400).room(0.8).size(0.9).gain(0.35)
`} />

### Hoover-ish lead (hard house / bounce)

The real hoover comes from a Roland Alpha Juno patch with pulse-width modulation and pitch bends. You can get the *idea* in Strudel: a detuned supersaw an octave stack, with a pitch envelope that swoops into each note, and drive.

<Strudel title="hoover-ish" code={`
  setcpm(145/4)
  hoover: note("<[a3 ~ ~ a3 ~ ~ g3 ~] [c4 ~ ~ a3 ~ ~ e3 ~]>")
    .add(note("0,12"))                 // octave stack
    .s("supersaw").unison(7).detune(0.5)
    .penv(-7).pattack(0.08)            // swoop up into each note
    .lpf(2600).distort(1.2).gain(0.4)
    .decay(0.3).sustain(0.6).release(0.1)
  kick: s("bd*4").bank("RolandTR909").distort(1)
`} />

## 5. Effects (a first look)

| Effect | Strudel | Use | Careful with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reverb | `.room(0.4).size(0.8)` | space, depth, breakdowns | never on bass or kick |
| Delay | `.delay(0.5).delaytime(3/16)` | echoes in time, fills gaps | keep delay time on the grid (1/8, 3/16) |
| Distortion | `.distort(1)` / `.shape(0.5)` | adds harmonics, aggression | gets loud fast |
| Bit crush | `.crush(6)` | lo-fi, rave grit | small doses |
